## Method and results
- **Sample**: Hamilton editorials, 1902-1929 (27 years). Table I caption uses December 1903 to November 1929. N = 255 editorials, 11 indeterminate and dropped (Q20), so 244 coded. One scorer, versus Cowles's five.
- **Benchmark**: Cowles market index, value-weighted with income, called "S&P". Commercial paper rates as the alternative asset. Monthly rebalancing using the last call in each month (Q28). Cowles used a 50/50 mix of Dow industrials and railroads.
- **Time in market (months)**: long 46 %, bills 38 %, short 16 % (Q21). By call count: long 54 %, neutral 24 %, short 22 % (Q22).

Table I, nonparametric (riskless rate 5 % p.a., market up or down versus riskless rate):

| | Market Up | Market Down | Sum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call Up | 74 | 56 | 130 |
| Call Down | 18 | 36 | 54 |
| Sum | 92 | 92 | |

- **Fisher's exact test statistic**: 8.74.
- **Henriksson-Merton test**: N1 = 92, N2 = 92, N = 184, n1 = 36, n2 = 18, n = 54, E(n1) = 27, standard deviation of n1 = 2.53, t = 3.56.

- **Bootstrap on returns (500 draws)**:
  - Annual return: null mean 5.32 %, t = 2.580, rank 0.995.
  - Sharpe ratio: null mean 0.045, t = 2.468, rank 0.990.
  - Jensen alpha: null mean -1.28 %, standard deviation 2.11 %, t = 2.518, rank 0.996.
- **Bootstrap on strategies (500 draws)**:
  - Annual return: null mean 5.12 %, t = 3.090, rank 1.00.
  - Sharpe ratio: null mean 0.023, t = 3.012, rank 1.00.
  - Jensen alpha: null mean -1.48 %, t = 2.844, rank 0.997.
- **Text inconsistency**: the text says "The actual return of 9.95 percent ranks above the 99th percentile," while the table shows 10.73 %.
- **Event study (81 trading days, Dow Industrials)**: bull +1.5 %, bear -1.74 %, neutral +0.21 % over the next 40 days. The two-tailed t-test gives p = 0.034 (Q33-Q35).
- **Stepwise regression of bear calls (Table III)**:
  - sixty-day Industrials return: -14.24 (t = -6.67).
  - sixty-day Transports return: -9.72 (t = -6.38).
  - thirty-day Industrials return: -3.70 (t = -2.70).
  - thirty-day Transports return: 6.14 (t = 3.18).
  - same-sign indicator: 0.12 (t = 1.30).
  - sixty-day return times same sign: 12.19 (t = 5.19).
- **Neural net in-sample (1902-1929)**: actual calls were 3,599 buy, 1,143 sell and 2,912 neutral. The net predicted 469 buy, 4,464 sell and 2,721 neutral.
- **Neural net out-of-sample (15 Sep 1930 to 1 Dec 1997, 17,457 days)**: 10,004 buy, 6,131 sell and 1,322 neutral.

## Notes
- This is a working-paper draft. The Journal of Finance version may differ.
- The paper cites Cowles as 1934, but it is 1933.
- Hamilton's calls were scored by one coder and are not Dow's own writing. The paper says "the Dow Theory was never written down by Charles Dow."
- Hamilton's calls were read from Rhea (1932) and Barron's.
- The paper lists only two signs, confirmation and lines. The three stages of bull and bear markets are separate.
- The text figure of 9.95 % conflicts with the table's 10.73 %.
- Out-of-sample signals come from a neural-net imitation of Hamilton's calls, not from mechanical Dow rules.
- The next-day strategy trades at the prior close, which the authors say may be an artefact (p.19).
- The out-of-sample benchmark excludes dividends, and cash earns zero interest.
- The 2019 NYU Stern and TheStreet figure of 4.4 points matches the geometric gap for the next-day strategy: 9.87 minus 5.48 is 4.39. That is my arithmetic, not the paper's statement.
