## Method and results
- **Sample**: Hamilton's Wall Street Journal editorials, December 1903 to December 1929 (26 years). Cowles also says 1904 to 1929 in the summary. N = 255 editorials, each scored bullish, bearish or doubtful by majority of five readers (Q4-Q6).
- **Trading rule**: bullish means long equal dollar amounts of the Dow Jones railroad and industrial stocks. Bearish means short them. Doubtful means out of the market.
- **Adjustments**: rights, dividends and splits via Rose tables, brokerage charges, and interest on idle funds (Q10, Q24).
- **Industrials**: 12 % p.a. for Hamilton against 15.5 % buy-and-hold, with a "normal" return of about 5 % (Q11, Q12, Q14).
- **Railroads**: 5.7 % against 7.7 % (Q15).
- **Time in market**: long 55 %, short 16 %, out 29 % (Q16).
- **Changes of position (industrials)**: 90 in total, 45 successful and 45 unsuccessful (Q21, Q26, Q27).
  - 29 bullish, 16 profitable.
  - 23 bearish, 10 profitable.
  - 38 withdrawals, 19 profitable.
- **Changes of position (railroads)**: 41 successful, 49 unsuccessful (Q22).
- **Period 1909-1914**: the hypothetical fund fell 7.8 % per year below a 5 % loan (Q23).
- **Statistics**: no Sharpe ratio, alpha, beta or p-values for Hamilton. Cowles uses probability and correlation tests only for the 16 services and 24 publications.
- **Other findings in the paper**: 16 financial services averaged -1.43 % against the market. 20 fire insurance companies averaged -1.20 %. 24 forecasters averaged about 4 % below random.

## Notes
- Brown, Goetzmann and Kumar cite this as "1934". The correct citation is July 1933.
- The "half the changes were profitable" sentence is Brown, Goetzmann and Kumar's paraphrase. Cowles gives 45 successful and 45 unsuccessful.
- The trading rule uses railroad and industrial stocks together, so it does not test the confirmation rule.
- Hamilton's calls were scored by five readers, which is a subjective step.
- The test does not adjust for risk, and Hamilton was out of the market 29 % of the time.
- Quote Q25 says 1904 to 1929, while Q11 says December 1903 to December 1929.
- A search-tool summary claimed Rhea (1933) reported a 29 % risk-adjusted return. That is unverified and not used.
