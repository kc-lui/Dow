## Verbatim quotes
Restated here only where needed for the index; each is identical in Nelson and Hamilton except for digits versus spelled-out numbers.

- Q1: "The market is always to be considered as having three movements, all going on at the same time. The first is the narrow movement from day to day. The second is the short swing, running from two weeks to a month or more; the third is the main movement covering at least four years in its duration." — Dec 19, 1900 (= Nelson Q2, Hamilton Q8)
- Q2: "Nothing is more certain than that the market has three well defined movements which fit into each other. The first is the daily variation due to local causes and the balance of buying or selling at that particular time. The secondary movement covers a period ranging from ten days to sixty days, averaging probably between thirty and forty days. The third move is the great swing covering from four to six years." — Jan 4, 1902 (= Nelson Q6; Hamilton Q6 reads "The third swing is the great move")
- Q3: "It is a bull period as long as the average of one high point exceeds that of previous high points. It is a bear period when the low point becomes lower than the previous low points." — Jan 4, 1902 (= Nelson Q8)
- Q4: "There come times when a stock with a good degree of activity will stay within a narrow range of prices, say 2 points, until there has formed quite a long horizontal line of these figures." — Jul 20, 1901 (= Nelson Q12, Hamilton Q10)
- Q5: "It seems to be a fact that a primary movement in the market will generally have a secondary movement in the opposite direction of at least three-eighths of the primary movement." — Jul 20, 1901 (= Nelson Q15, Hamilton Q11)
- Q6: "Bull markets and bear markets run four and five years at a time." — undated, Nelson Ch. VI (= Nelson Q1)

## Notes
- Dow's own statements are the only ones in this corpus that give "four to six years" and "three-eighths"; both were explicitly revised by Hamilton (primary "rarely three years and oftener less than two"; counter-movement often under forty days) and replaced by Rhea (33-66 %, three weeks to as many months).
- Dates are second-hand (Hamilton 1922; Schannep 2008 gives Dec 19, 1900 and Jan 4, 1902). Nelson prints no dates. Brown, Goetzmann and Kumar note that Dow never wrote the theory down.
- Dow's retracement and line figures are in points for individual stocks, not in percent for the averages.
- Schannep also attributes to Hamilton editorials the secondary ranges "20 days to 60 days" (Sep 17, 1904), "one month to three months" (Feb 26, 1909) and "a few days to many weeks" (Feb 11, 1922); those are Hamilton, not Dow, and are not in the 1922 book.
