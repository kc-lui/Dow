## Verbatim quotes
- Q1: "Basic Tenets To get back to the Dow Theory itself here are its basic tenets : 1. The Averages Discount Everything ( except " Acts of God " ) — Because they reflect the combined market" — 8th ed. (aEXDEl9RpGIC) snippet, Ch. 3
- Q2: "three weeks to three months, and which may retrace one-third to two-thirds of the previous Primary Advance or" — 11th ed. (MDgPEAAAQBAJ) glossary snippet, entry on Secondary/Intermediate trend
- Q3: "MINOR TREND - In Edwards and Magee , the term Minor refers to brief fluctuations ( usu- ally less than six days and rarely longer than three weeks ) that , in total , make up the Intermediate Trend ." — 10th ed. (LB9XIvh9yuIC) glossary snippet
- Q4: "The two Averages must confirm: This is the most-often questioned and the most difficult to rationalize of all the Dow principles." — 11th ed. (cVJmDwAAQBAJ) snippet, Ch. 3
- Q5: "Only Closing Prices Used - Dow Theory pays no attention to any extreme highs" — 1992 ed. (deUJAQAAMAAJ) snippet, Ch. 3 tenet 11
- Q6: "Trend Should Be Assumed to Continue in Effect Until Such Time as Its Reversal Has Been Definitely Sig- naled - This" — 1992 ed. snippet, Ch. 3 tenet heading
- Q7: "Generally speaking, the longer the Line (in duration) and the narrower or more compact its price range, the greater the significance of its ultimate breakout." — 10th ed. snippet, Ch. 3
- Q8: "too late " -This is a more valid objection ." — 1954 ed. (EtJRAQAAMAAJ) snippet, Ch. V "The Dow Theory's Defects"

Other snippet content, paraphrased: primary trends last a year or more and produce appreciation or depreciation of more than 20 % (1992 ed.); the 11th ed. says a line's 5 % limit is the ordinary width and that the direction of breakout cannot be known in advance; the 8th ed. says a break above a line is bullish and below is bearish; the 1992 ed. says a Dow line resembles the Rectangle pattern of individual stocks; the 11th ed. says each Secondary Reaction stopping above the previous reaction low marks an up primary trend; the 1992 ed. says volume affords only collateral evidence and signals come from price alone. One secondary source (T. H. Stewart 1996) reads Edwards and Magee as saying a secondary "must last at least three weeks and retrace at least a third".

## Notes
- All content is from Google Books search snippets, which are fragments spanning several editions (1954 to 2018). Wording differs slightly between editions (for example "Averages Discount Everything" versus "Averages discount everything"). Edition and id are given per quote.
- The duration and retracement figures (three weeks to three months, one-third to two-thirds, less than six days) are from the glossary of the 10th and 11th editions, which was written by Bassetti, not from Edwards and Magee's 1948 text; the 1992 edition snippet confirms the minor-trend wording appears in the main text too.
- The "20 %" primary-trend figure and the "5 % limit" for lines come from snippets too short to quote cleanly; treat them as reported, not verified.
- The twelve-tenet enumeration is Edwards and Magee's reorganisation of Rhea; compare rhea-1932-the-dow-theory for the original formulation (33-66 %, 3 % rally filter, 5 % lines).
- Chapter 5 of the 10th and 11th editions (Bassetti) proposes replacing Dow Theory with a "Basing Points Procedure", a criticism in itself.
- No pirated PDF was used; several search hits (archive.org upload, scribd, slideshare, pbookshop) were deliberately not opened.
