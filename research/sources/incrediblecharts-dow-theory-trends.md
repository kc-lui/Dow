- Q1: "Nothing is more certain than that the market has three well defined movements which fit into each other." — Three movements
- Q2: "The first is the daily variation due to local causes and the balance of buying and selling at that particular time." — Three movements
- Q3: "The third move is the great swing covering from four to six years." — Three movements
- Q4: "A series of successive higher highs and higher lows." — Bull trend
- Q5: "A series of successive lower highs and lower lows." — Bear trend
- Q6: "For practical purposes, only accept large corrections as trend changes in the primary trend." — Reversals
- Q7: "Secondary reactions may take the shape of a line: a narrow consolidation with a range of 5% or less." — Lines
- Q8: "Bull markets commence with reviving confidence as business conditions improve." — Phases
- Q9: "Bear markets start with abandonment of the hopes and expectations that sustained inflated prices." — Phases
- Q10: "Increased volume on declines and dull activity on rallies provide additional evidence of an overbought market." — Volume
- Q11: "A bear market commences when a bear trend on one average is confirmed by the start of a bear trend on the other average." — Confirmation
- Q12: "It is not an infallible system for beating the market." — Limitation, attributed to Robert Rhea
- Q13: "Dow recognized that trends changed when the pattern of peaks and troughs reversed." — Reversals
- Q14: "A trend should always be treated as intact until there is a clear signal that the opposite trend has started." — Reversals
- Q15: "A bull market starts when a bull trend on one average is confirmed by the other average commencing a bull trend." — Confirmation
- Q16: "Conversely, lack of activity on declines and increased volume during rallies indicate an oversold market." — Volume
