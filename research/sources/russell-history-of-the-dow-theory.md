## Verbatim quotes
- Q1: "Dow's good friend, S.A. Nelson, published 15 of Dow's Wall Street Journal editorials in a little volume entitled, "The ABC of Stock Speculation."" — p.1
- Q2: "A footnote at the bottom of each chapter refers to the editorial as "Dow's Theory." But Dow himself never once used the term." — p.1
- Q3: "Hamilton wrote a brilliant series of 252 editorials." — p.1
- Q4: "But Hamilton also presented his own views on Dow Theory, and it was Hamilton who first defined the confirmation principle of the Averages." — p.2
- Q5: "Years earlier Rhea had written that in a bull market, secondary reactions tend to retrace one-third to two-thirds of the preceding uncorrected primary advance while tending to last three weeks to three months." — p.5
- Q6: "Although Hamilton and Rhea took careful note of the secondary reactions in bull and bear markets, Schaefer advised his subscribers to ignore these "temporary reactions," and to remain invested in harmony with the primary trend of the market." — p.4
- Q7: "When the Averages broke through their preceding secondary lows, many orthodox Dow Theorists, who relied almost totally (as most do today) on the pattern of the Averages, proclaimed that a bear market had started." — p.5
- Q8: "The Dow Theory (actually it is a set of observations) has basically to do with buying great values and selling those values when they become overpriced." — p.1

Paraphrased: Russell quotes Schaefer quoting Dow that "values determine prices in the long run" and that a patient investor who studies values "will likely make money in stocks"; Schaefer's 1949 report put Dow's priorities as values first, economic conditions second, and the action of both averages third; Schaefer's "New Dow Theory" put "no restrictions whatever on the duration or extent of the secondary or intermediate trend"; Russell's 50 % Principle example: 1953 low 255.49 to 1956 high 521.05 gives a halfway level of 388.27, and the 1957 low of 419.79 held above it, which Russell read as bullish.

## Notes
- The URL label "Poitras, Dow Theory history paper" is wrong. The PDF is Richard Russell's Dow Theory Letters essay, hosted on Poitras's SFU course page. No Poitras text is in the file. An academic Poitras paper on Dow Theory history, if it exists, was not found at this URL.
- Factual slips in Russell: Hamilton's book is dated 1926 (it was 1922, with later printings to 1926); Hamilton's editorial is called "The Turn of the Tide" (title is "A Turn in the Tide"); Nelson's preface credits Chapters IV to XX (17 chapters) to Dow, not 15, though the "Dow's Theory" footnote appears on about 14 chapters (see nelson-1903-abc-of-stock-speculation).
- Rhea's secondary rule appears here only as Russell's paraphrase (Q5); for Rhea's wording see rhea-1932-the-dow-theory.
- Russell's own method (values, 50 % Principle, Primary Trend Index) is not classical Dow Theory; the existing note russell-dow-theory-letters covers other Russell statements from a different source (FNArena), so the two notes do not overlap in text.
- Useful as a lineage document (Dow, Hamilton, Rhea, Schaefer, Russell) and for the orthodox versus Schaefer split over whether a break of secondary lows alone defines a bear market.
